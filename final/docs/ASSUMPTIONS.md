# Budget assumptions and references

The three dollar amounts below are editable scenario parameters. The external references provide context for their definitions. No source estimates these exact amounts for the project population.

| Parameter | Baseline and rationale | Evidence and limits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Essential spending | $1,500/month for a simple starting budget, with rent, loan payments and childcare modeled separately. Sensitivity uses ±20%. | The [Living Wage Calculator methodology](https://livingwage.mit.edu/pages/methodology) identifies local costs such as food, healthcare, communications and transport. Replacing our baseline with a local estimate would require selecting the household type, geography and cost components and excluding costs modeled elsewhere. |
| Savings target | $250/month sets the comparison standard for a budget with room to save. It is deducted when calculating feasibility. | The [CFPB emergency savings guide](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/an-essential-guide-to-building-an-emergency-fund/) describes why households save and why targets vary by circumstance. |
| Family support | $0/month is the independence condition. $500/month is the assisted scenario used to compare a fixed cash transfer. | The [CFPB family support guide](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/consumer-tools/educator-tools/adult-financial-education/tips-for-managing-family-lending-and-borrowing/) documents informal support as a mechanism, without establishing $500 as an average or an entitlement. |

Other defaults include a 10-year loan term at 6.39%, approximate 2025 single-filer taxes, 30% of gross income as a rent burden comparison, $20,389/$25,000 in institution-level median/P75 completer debt and about $1,795/month for statewide center-based childcare. See outputs/assumptions.json for the recorded model configuration and education-data provenance.
