# From College to Independence: Who Can Afford to Start Adult Life in the Bay Area?

### Problem Statement
A college degree is often presented as a pathway to financial independence. However, the transition from education to independent adulthood is shaped not only by earnings, but also by student debt, entry-level job opportunities, housing costs, and unequal financial starting points.

### Research Goal
Our project investigates the financial barriers facing new college graduates who hope to live and work in the San Francisco Bay Area. Rather than predicting individual employment outcomes, we evaluate financial independence under different realistic scenarios.

### Data Sources
- **College Affordability & Outcomes:** Educational costs, student debt, graduation outcomes, and affordability inequalities.
- **Indeed Job Listings:** Entry-level job requirements, salary transparency, and advertised wages.
- **Zillow Housing:** Rental prices across Bay Area cities.

### Methodology
We analyze college affordability using Bay Area institutions with national benchmarks, examine entry-level employment and rental conditions, and develop a scenario-based financial independence simulator. The tool allows users to explore how occupation, location, student debt, housing arrangements, and financial support influence their estimated monthly budget.

### Social Good Impact
Our goal is to make the structural barriers to financial independence more visible and understandable, particularly for graduates with limited financial resources, student debt, or additional family responsibilities.

The resulting dashboard can help prospective graduates explore financial trade-offs and provide educators, community organizations, and policymakers with evidence about where affordability gaps are especially pronounced.

### Limitations
The three datasets are not linked at the individual level. Therefore, the project does not predict graduate employment probabilities or actual individual financial outcomes. Its findings represent descriptive comparisons and scenario-based estimates under explicit assumptions.
